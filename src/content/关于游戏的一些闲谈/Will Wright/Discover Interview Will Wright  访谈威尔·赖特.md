The master of the computer god game tackles alien life and dreams up a world that would make Darwin drool.  
这位电脑“上帝游戏”大师把目光投向外星生命，并构想出一个足以让达尔文垂涎的世界。

Written by Alan Burdick  
作者：艾伦·伯迪克

2006-08-01 | 9 min read  
2006 年 8 月 1 日｜阅读约 9 分钟

Will Wright followed a typically eccentric path into computer-game design: some college classes in computer science and architecture, a few homemade robots, no university degree. A deep interest in science, however, infuses all his creations. SimAnt, in which players try to corral an ant colony into conquering a suburban home, was modeled on the insights of ant expert Edward O. Wilson. For SimEarth, a global-ecosystem game, Wright consulted with biologist James Lovelock, originator of the Gaia “Earth as organism” hypothesis. SimCity was inspired by urban-dynamics models developed by MIT scientist Jay Forrester. The Sims games, beneath their animated-dollhouse exteriors, are time-management experiments, based in part on a trove of data gathered by sociologist John Robinson on how Americans spend their hours.

威尔·赖特走上电脑游戏设计这条路的方式，颇有几分典型的离经叛道：上过一些计算机科学和建筑学课程，自己做过几台机器人，却没有取得大学学位。不过，对科学的浓厚兴趣始终贯穿于他的所有作品之中。《模拟蚂蚁》中，玩家要引导蚁群攻占一栋郊区住宅；它的设计参考了蚂蚁研究专家爱德华·O·威尔逊的见解。为了制作全球生态系统游戏《模拟地球》，赖特曾请教生物学家詹姆斯·洛夫洛克——“盖亚假说”，即“地球是一个有机体”这一观点的提出者。《模拟城市》则受到麻省理工学院科学家杰伊·福雷斯特所开发的城市动力学模型启发。《模拟人生》系列在动画玩具屋般的外表之下，实际上是一种时间管理实验，部分依据来自社会学家约翰·罗宾逊收集的大量资料：美国人究竟如何支配自己的时间。

Wright’s next game, Spore, due out next year, simulates the entire cosmos; he refers to it jokingly as SimEverything. The player starts as a microbe in a cell-eat-cell world and gradually advances onto land, evolves sentience, develops culture, forms tribes, cities, and civilizations, and finally acquires the ability to move freely through a breathtakingly vast universe of planets, stars, and galaxies. Everything is malleable: A player can create a creature with, say, 3 legs and 15 eyes and stretch it like clay. The animating software then figures out how best to make it walk, run, and stalk prey. A player can create ringed planets and watch the moons orbit leisurely for hours. Meanwhile, the many worlds in your Spore cosmos are pollinated automatically from an online database of plants and animals created by other players.

赖特的下一部作品《孢子》预计明年发售，它要模拟整个宇宙；他半开玩笑地把它称为“模拟一切”。玩家从一个在细胞彼此吞食的世界里求生的微生物开始，逐步登陆陆地，进化出感知能力，发展文化，建立部落、城市与文明，最后获得在由行星、恒星和星系构成的浩瀚宇宙中自由穿行的能力。一切都可以塑造：玩家可以创造一种有三条腿、十五只眼睛的生物，像拉扯橡皮泥一样改变它的形态。动画软件随后会自行计算，怎样让它最适合行走、奔跑和追捕猎物。玩家也可以创造带有行星环的星球，闲看卫星绕着它缓慢运行数小时。与此同时，你的《孢子》宇宙中那无数个世界，会自动从其他玩家创建的在线动植物数据库中获得“授粉”。

“As you play, you create the elements of the universe, which are used to populate other players’ worlds,” Wright says. “In a sense, you’re creating the universe for other players. We’re making the player the game designer.”

“在游戏过程中，你会创造宇宙的各种元素，而这些元素会被用来充实其他玩家的世界。”赖特说，“从某种意义上讲，你是在替其他玩家创造宇宙。我们是在让玩家成为游戏设计师。”

Through your games, you come across as a guy who’s trying to decipher the natural world bit by bit, through computer simulations.

从你的游戏里看，你给人的感觉像是一个试图借助计算机模拟，一点一点解读自然世界的人。

That’s not far off. When I was a kid, I liked taking things apart to see how they worked. Computer simulation is similar, it’s reductionist; you’ve got these parts, you want to see how they interact, so you build a model and compare it to the real world. When you formulate a model, you quickly see your misperceptions. That’s the value of simulation in science, to spotlight our ignorance.

这说得不算离谱。我小时候喜欢把东西拆开，看看它们是怎么运作的。计算机模拟也很相似，它是一种还原论的方法：你有这些部件，想知道它们如何相互作用，于是就建立一个模型，再把它与现实世界比较。当你构建出模型，很快就会发现自己原先的误解。模拟在科学中的价值就在这里：它把我们的无知照亮出来。

Modeling is one of the things that led to an understanding of chaos theory. Back in the 1960s, Dennis and Donella Meadows, a husband and wife team, tried to model the world in terms of things like population, food production, standard of living, and so on to get some sense of where the world was going. When they ran their model, it basically showed the whole world population crashing—quickly, by 1985, according to them. Of course, that didn’t come true.

建模是促成人们理解混沌理论的因素之一。20 世纪 60 年代，丹尼斯·梅多斯和多内拉·梅多斯夫妇试图建立一个世界模型，将人口、粮食产量、生活水平等因素纳入其中，借此判断世界会走向何方。模型运行的结果基本显示，全球人口会迅速崩溃——按他们的预测，到 1985 年便会发生。当然，现实并没有如此发展。

Looking back, it became clear that just a couple of variables were off by a few percent and got very amplified. The scientists didn’t foresee the green revolution in agriculture—the use of fertilizers and pesticides. So their food production numbers were just a bit low, but it compounded year after year. One little thing off a little bit can have a huge impact on the eventual destination.

回头看就会明白，只是几个变量偏离了几个百分点，后来便被大幅放大。科学家当时没有预见到农业中的绿色革命——化肥和农药的广泛使用。因此，他们对粮食产量的估计仅仅低了一点点，但这个偏差一年一年累积下去。某个小地方稍有偏差，最后就可能极大地影响系统抵达的终点。

What were you doing at age 10 that steered you toward game design?

你十岁的时候在做些什么，后来把你引向了游戏设计？

Building a lot of models—plastic, wood, whatever. That evolved into making things with motors, and that evolved into robots. Robots got me into computers. One of my favorite robots was one called Mr. Rogers. I built it when I was about 20. It had three wheels and an ultrasonic sensor for mapping the room and was attached to an Apple II. I still love robots; it’s kind of a background hobby. My daughter, Cassidy—she’s 19, she’s in art school—was doing Robot Wars and BattleBots with me for many years.

做许多模型——塑料的、木头的，什么材料都有。后来我开始做带马达的东西，再后来就做起机器人来。机器人把我带进了计算机世界。我最喜欢的一台机器人叫“罗杰斯先生”，大约二十岁时做的。它有三只轮子，还有一个用来绘制房间地图的超声波传感器，连接着一台 Apple II 电脑。我现在仍然喜欢机器人；它算是一项长期的业余爱好。我女儿卡西迪——她十九岁，在艺术学校读书——多年来一直和我一起参加 Robot Wars 和 BattleBots 的比赛。

Spore takes its cue from astrobiology, both in its spatial sweep—from microbiology to galaxies—and in the interplanetary spread of life. What turned you on to the subject?

《孢子》从天体生物学中获得灵感：无论是它从微生物延展到星系的空间尺度，还是生命跨行星传播的设想。是什么让你对这个主题产生兴趣？

Well, I’ve always had an interest in the SETI program, which led me to astrobiology and to Drake’s equation. Drake’s equation is simple. Basically, you take the average number of stars in the galaxy and you ask what percentage have habitable planets.

我一直对 SETI，即搜寻地外文明计划，很感兴趣；它把我带进了天体生物学，也带到了德雷克方程。德雷克方程很简单。基本上，你先从银河系恒星的平均数量出发，再问：其中有多少比例拥有适合居住的行星？

Then you ask what percentage of those couple of planets does life arise on? And on what percentage of those is the life intelligent? What’s the average life span of that civilization? You crunch all those numbers together and get one that tells you how many intelligent species are out there asking themselves the same question. For some reason, most of these models leave out panspermia [the theory that life may have originated elsewhere in the cosmos]; I love to think panspermia’s gotten short shrift.

接着你再问：这些行星中，有多少比例会产生生命？其中又有多少比例会产生智慧生命？这些文明的平均寿命有多长？把这些数字统统算在一起，就会得出一个结果：宇宙中有多少智慧物种正在问自己同样的问题。不知为什么，大多数模型都忽略了泛生论——即生命可能起源于宇宙其他地方的理论；我喜欢认为，泛生论一直没有得到应有的重视。

Anyway, all the factors lead back to how unique we are. Stars and galaxies are complex and interesting, but they’re still nowhere near as complex as life.

无论如何，所有这些因素最后都会回到一个问题：我们究竟有多么独特。恒星和星系当然复杂而有趣，但与生命相比，它们仍远远谈不上同样复杂。

One thing that interests me is that all the factors in Drake’s equation map to different size scales. It’s almost like an index into science at different scales: chemistry, biology, sociology. As humans we’re stuck at the scale of our bodies, but there are all these different levels above and below us; each one has its own dynamics, its own processes, its own timescale. I’ve always been intrigued by Charles and Ray Eames’s Powers of Ten book and movie.

让我感兴趣的一点是，德雷克方程中的全部因素对应着不同的尺度。它几乎像是一份通往不同尺度科学领域的索引：化学、生物学、社会学。作为人类，我们被局限在自身身体的尺度上；但在我们之上和之下，还有许多不同的层级，每一层都有自己的动力、自己的过程、自己的时间尺度。我一直很着迷于查尔斯和雷·伊姆斯的《十的力量》一书和同名影片。

They really tried to give an overall sense of where we are in the universe, to give some perspective on the history of life. That awareness can make you feel insignificant. But in some sense, it’s also the reverse. If we’re the only life around, what an incredible responsibility! It’s humbling and deeply empowering at the same time.

他们真正试图呈现的是：我们在宇宙中处于什么位置，并由此给生命的历史建立一种视角。这样的认识会让你感到自身渺小。但从某种意义上说，情况也恰恰相反。要是我们是周围唯一的生命，那将意味着多么惊人的责任！它既令人谦卑，也深深赋予人力量。

So Spore is an existential game?

所以，《孢子》是一款关于存在主义的游戏？

One of my original goals was to give players the equivalent of a drug-induced epiphany. I’ve been surprised, given Spore’s epic scale, that it has such broad appeal—that the average person finds some meaning in it. Of course, every player finds a different meaning: how big the universe is, or the existence of different timescales, or how precious life is. The important thing is getting people to step back and enjoy the view.

我最初的目标之一，是让玩家获得一种近似于药物引发的顿悟。考虑到《孢子》的宏大尺度，它居然能拥有如此广泛的吸引力，这让我很意外——普通人也能从中发现某种意义。当然，每个玩家找到的意义各不相同：宇宙有多么浩瀚，不同时间尺度的存在，或者生命本身有多么珍贵。重要的是让人退后一步，欣赏眼前的景象。

The Spore universe plays like a planetarium show; you’ve clearly worked hard to model orbital and galactic motions accurately.

《孢子》的宇宙像一场天文馆表演；显然，你们花了很多功夫，来准确模拟轨道和星系运动。

You should see all the stuff that’s not in the game! We did a huge number of prototypes, modeling almost anything you can imagine, from autocatalytic chemistry to the dynamics of interstellar gases. For a brief while, we considered making gas giants playable, but not having a solid surface makes game play difficult.

你应该看看那些最后没有进入游戏的东西！我们做了大量原型，几乎模拟了任何你能想象到的内容，从自催化化学到星际气体动力学。我们一度考虑过让气态巨行星也成为可以游玩的对象，但没有固体表面，会让玩法变得很困难。

I’m told you collect artifacts from the Russian space program.

我听说你收藏俄罗斯航天计划的文物。

I’ve always had a fascination with it. I’m impressed by their approach and the success they’ve had compared to NASA. And they’ve done it at one-fifth the cost. These days you have to hire the Russians to get you into space, not NASA. I like to collect their stuff, take it apart, see how it works. It’s incredibly durable, and cheap. I’ve got control panels from the Mir space station and the complete interior of a Soyuz spacecraft.

我一直对它很着迷。和 NASA 相比，我对他们的方法以及他们取得的成果印象深刻。而且他们只花了五分之一的成本。如今，想把人送上太空，你得雇俄罗斯人，而不是 NASA。我喜欢收集他们的东西，把它们拆开，看看是怎么运作的。这些东西耐用得惊人，而且便宜。我有和平号空间站的控制面板，也收藏着联盟号飞船的一整套内部舱体。

I’m going to Russia next week, actually, to Star City and some other places. A lot of the coolest stuff is down in the basements of these aerospace corporations. I’m going with several friends; it’s sort of a space junket.

实际上，我下周就要去俄罗斯，去星城和其他一些地方。很多最酷的东西，都藏在这些航天公司的地下室里。我会和几个朋友一起去，算是一趟太空主题的旅行。

Would you ever go up in space?

你会去太空吗？

Oh, sure, I’d do it under the right circumstances. But not $20 million to fly in the Soyuz.

当然，在条件合适的情况下我会去。但我不会花两千万美元坐联盟号飞船上天。

Do you play computer games besides the ones you design?

除了你自己设计的游戏，你还玩别的电脑游戏吗？

Oh, yeah. I spend maybe five hours a week playing games. On the PC, I still play Battlefield 2 and Advance War on my old Game Boy. Lately I’ve been into Guitar Hero. It’s a game for PlayStation 2 that comes with a guitar controller, which has buttons on its frets instead of strings. You try to play along to real rock songs, and there’s this whole little audience on-screen that’ll boo you off the stage if you stink.

当然。我每周大概会花五个小时玩游戏。在电脑上，我还玩《战地 2》；在那台老 Game Boy 上，我会玩《高级战争》。最近我很迷《吉他英雄》。那是 PlayStation 2 上的一款游戏，附带一个吉他控制器；它的品格位置不是琴弦，而是按钮。你要试着跟着真实的摇滚歌曲演奏，屏幕上还有一群小观众：要是你弹得太差，他们就会把你嘘下台。

When you get it right, it’s really satisfying.

当你做对了，感觉真的很满足。

What makes a game compelling to you?

对你来说，什么让一款游戏具有吸引力？

In the kinds of games I focus on, I’m interested in amplifying the players’ natural abilities. I want a player to feel surprised: “Wow, I made this thing!” Then, because you feel ownership over it, you start feeling things like pride—or even guilt if you run the situation badly. People talk about how games don’t have the emotional impact of movies. I think they do—they just have a different palette. I never felt pride, or guilt, watching a movie.

==在我专注的那类游戏里，我感兴趣的是放大玩家自身的能力。我希望玩家会感到惊讶：“哇，这东西是我做出来的！”然后，因为你对它有一种所有权，就会生出自豪之类的情绪——要是你把局面经营得一团糟，甚至还会有愧疚感。人们常说，游戏没有电影那样的情感冲击力。我认为它们有，只是调色板不同。看电影时，我从来没有感受过自豪或愧疚。==

A lot of what makes things fun generally is people challenging themselves, learning new patterns. You’re building a model in your head that will help you predict what the system’s going to do and enable you to perform in that system more accurately. That’s why kids play, I think. From a very early age, that’s how we relate to the world. We look for patterns, we poke and prod: If I do this, what happens? That’s how we learn causality.

==一般来说，许多乐趣来自人们挑战自己、学习新的模式。你在脑中建立一个模型，它帮助你预测系统接下来会做什么，也让你能更准确地在这个系统中行动。我想，这就是孩子为什么会玩。从很小的时候起，我们就是这样和世界发生关系的。我们寻找规律，不断试探：如果我这样做，会发生什么？我们就是这样学会因果关系的。==

So games are fun because they allow us to play with time?

所以，游戏之所以有趣，是因为它们让我们能玩弄时间？

Partly. You can think of games almost as time machines. They allow us to explore the possibility space around a given starting point. You can hit Start Over and do the Groundhog Day thing: Relive the same day and try doing this, replay the same day and try doing that. You can control time in a way that you can never do in real life and get some sense of how chaotic a system can be.

部分是这样。你几乎可以把游戏看成时间机器。它们允许我们探索某个特定起点周围的可能性空间。你可以按下“重新开始”，像《土拨鼠之日》那样：重过同一天，试着做这件事；再重过同一天，试着做另一件事。你可以用一种现实生活里永远做不到的方式控制时间，并由此体会一个系统能有多么混沌。

Storytelling is the same way. Say I’m a caveman and I almost get killed by a tiger. I can come tell you that I left my cave and a tiger almost got me. I’m sharing an experience and can now influence your behavior. Next time you leave the cave, you’ll look out for the tiger. That’s a time machine for experience—lessons that we might learn.

讲故事也是一样。假设我是一个穴居人，差点被老虎杀死。我可以回来告诉你：我离开洞穴后，差点被一只老虎害死。我分享了一段经历，于是就能影响你的行为。下一次你离开洞穴时，就会提防老虎。这是一台为经验而存在的时间机器——它把我们本可能从中学到的教训带了回来。

You’ve collected and analyzed thousands of hours of data gathered from people who play The Sims online. To what extent is playing The Sims a behavioral experiment?

你收集并分析了数千小时来自《模拟人生》线上玩家的数据。玩《模拟人生》在多大程度上算是一种行为实验？

It’s an interesting kind of Rorschach test. The way in which people play the game says a lot about their personal interests and creativity. Some focus on giving their Sims skills, climbing the career ladder, building McMansions. Others focus on romance or building a family. Others are into creating a cast of characters and saving it on the Web; for them The Sims is more like a set of actors and sets with which to tell stories. It’s up to you to decide how you want to make your Sim happy.

它是一种很有意思的罗夏墨迹测验。人们玩游戏的方式，很能说明他们个人的兴趣和创造力。有些人专注于培养模拟市民的技能、攀升职业阶梯、建造豪宅。另一些人关注爱情或组建家庭。还有些人喜欢创造一批角色，再把它们保存在网上；对他们来说，《模拟人生》更像一套演员与布景，用来讲述故事。你要怎样让自己的模拟市民幸福，完全由你决定。

SimCity is like that as well. We don’t tell you that you have to build a big city, or a happy city, or a clean city; people come up with their own goal state that has a lot to do with their own value system. The game almost asks them, OK, what do you think a good city is? Or a good life?

《模拟城市》也是如此。我们不会告诉你，必须建一座大城市、一座幸福的城市，或者一座干净的城市；玩家会提出自己的目标状态，而那和他们自身的价值体系密切相关。游戏几乎是在问他们：好吧，你觉得一座好城市是什么？或者，一种好生活是什么？

You’ve modeled planetary dynamics, ant colonies, even the way players play your games. What’s left?

你已经模拟过行星动力学、蚁群，甚至模拟过玩家游玩你游戏的方式。还有什么没被模拟？

Do you know about fitness landscapes? It’s this idea that you can map evolutionary fitness. If you were this genetic combination, you’d be this fit. If you were that genetic combination, you’d be that fit. Any given population is basically climbing a fitness landscape.

你知道“适应度景观”吗？它是一种观念：你可以绘制进化适应度的地图。要是你拥有这种基因组合，适应度会是这个水平；要是你拥有另一种基因组合，适应度又会是另一个水平。任何特定种群，本质上都在攀爬一张适应度景观。

It’s cross-correlated: The shape of the landscape is dependent on what all the organisms are doing, so even as an organism evolves, the landscape is always changing.

它们彼此交叉关联：景观的形状取决于所有生物体都在做什么，所以即使某个生物在进化，景观本身也始终在变化。

I did some modeling of this—fairly long-term models of creatures evolving on different landscapes. Interestingly, the results I got were very similar to punctuated equilibrium [an evolutionary theory championed by Niles Eldridge and Stephen Jay Gould]. You’d see regions of stability for long periods of time, then diversity would go up, then suddenly the whole system would go into chaos, you’d have this mass die-off, and then it would go back up pretty rapidly.

我对这个做过一些建模——相当长期地模拟生物在不同景观中的演化。有趣的是，我得到的结果和间断平衡理论非常相似——那是一种由奈尔斯·埃尔德里奇和斯蒂芬·杰伊·古尔德倡导的进化理论。你会看到某些区域长期保持稳定，接着多样性上升，然后整个系统忽然陷入混沌，发生大规模死亡，之后又会很快恢复上升。

Yeah, pretty much just for fun. I get really into biology. I find it more and more fascinating, especially macroevolutionary stuff. Actually, I think the idea of evolution is one that a lot of people have a hard time wrapping their minds around. They think, oh, you’ve got this one mutation and then the creature is a little bit better at seeing, therefore it survives.

对，基本上就是为了好玩。我真的很沉迷生物学。我发现它越来越迷人，尤其是宏观进化方面的内容。实际上，我觉得进化这个概念很难让许多人真正理解。他们会想：噢，出现了一次突变，这个生物的视力就稍微好一点，因此它便活了下来。

But, in fact, it’s much more of a numbers game: You have thousands of creatures that have a slightly better chance of seeing, and statistically they survive 1 percent better. People aren’t used to dealing with the numbers and the timescales involved. But once you look at it from that point of view, evolution just seems so much more plausible. It makes perfect sense.

但实际上，它更像是一场数字游戏：有成千上万的生物，它们看得更清楚的概率略高一点，而从统计上说，它们的存活率高出百分之一。人们并不习惯处理其中涉及的数字和时间尺度。但一旦从这个角度去看，进化就显得可信得多。它完全说得通。

维基百科将《孢子》形容为一款“目的论进化”游戏。你认为这款游戏会把自然选择的概念带给大众吗？

You can look at it in a number of ways. What’s ironic, really, is it’s intelligent design. As a player, you go through an arc of being this lowly little cell, being attacked by pond scum, to eventually becoming a god. At the godlike level, you can almost do the whole creationist thing if you want: “I will create a planet; I will create species; I will put them on the planet.” But you’re a god without a whole lot of foresight. You put all this stuff on a planet, and it might go kerflooey.

这个问题可以从许多角度看。真正讽刺的是，它其实是一种智能设计。作为玩家，你会经历一段弧线：从一个卑微的小细胞，被池塘里的污泥生物攻击，最后成为神。在神一般的层级上，只要你愿意，几乎可以做一整套创世论式的事情：“我要创造一颗行星；我要创造物种；我要把它们放在这颗行星上。”但你是一个并没有太多预见能力的神。你把这些东西放到一颗行星上，它们可能就会彻底失控。

You might make a really badly balanced ecosystem. You’re not necessarily an omnipotent god. That’s even more fun. You have these godlike powers, yet the repercussions of them become totally unpredictable to you.

你可能会造出一个严重失衡的生态系统。你未必是全能的神。这反而更有趣。你拥有神一般的力量，但这些力量的后果，对你来说却会变得完全不可预测。
