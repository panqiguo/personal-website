For almost 30 years, Will Wright's creations have attracted people who never played video games, enthralling hardcore fans while making rabid players out of novices. The secret: in Wright's worlds, there is no win or lose -- just the game.  
近三十年来，威尔·莱特的作品吸引了许多原本从不玩电子游戏的人，也令硬核玩家着迷，并把新手变成热衷其中的玩家。秘诀在于：在莱特创造的世界里，没有输赢，只有游戏本身。

Will Wright with a copy of Sim Copter  
威尔·莱特与一盒《模拟直升机》合影

He's best known for creating the Sim franchise. These unlikely blockbusters -- more than 180 million sold so far -- drew on the works of arcane theorists, urban planners and astrophysicists, yet they were addictive. They thrived thanks to what Wright calls possibility space: the scope of actions a player can take. Most games give a narrow possibility space: do you want to kill bad guys with bullets or grenades? Take the door on the right, or the one on the left?  
他最广为人知的身份，是“模拟”系列的创始人。这些原本看似不太可能成为大作的游戏，至今销量已超过 1.8 亿份。它们借鉴了深奥理论家的研究、城市规划师的思路和天体物理学家的知识，却依然令人上瘾。它们之所以成功，靠的是莱特所谓的“可能性空间”：也就是玩家能够采取行动的范围。多数游戏提供的可能性空间很狭窄：你是想用子弹杀掉坏人，还是用手榴弹？走右边那扇门，还是左边那扇门？

Wright and his team at Maxis, the development studio he cofounded in 1987, blew past those constraints, creating an infinitely flexible world limited only by the skill and imagination of the player. In Wright's best work, players have so much leeway to determine their objectives that the distinction between player and designer blurs.  
莱特和他的团队突破了这些限制。1987 年，他共同创办了游戏开发工作室 Maxis；在那里，他们创造出一个几乎无限灵活的世界，唯一的限制来自玩家自身的技巧与想象力。在莱特最出色的作品中，玩家拥有充分的自由来决定自己的目标，以至于玩家和设计师之间的界线都变得模糊。

In 2009, after more than 20 years at Maxis, Wright stepped down to form Stupid Fun Club, an entertainment-development think tank. He met Wired in Stupid Fun's Berkeley studio to review his career, hint about upcoming projects, and speculate about what the future holds for us all -- gamers or not.  
2009 年，在 Maxis 工作二十多年后，莱特离开公司，成立了 Stupid Fun Club——一家专注于娱乐开发的创意智库。他在 Stupid Fun 位于伯克利的工作室接受《连线》采访，回顾了自己的职业生涯，透露了一些未来项目的线索，也谈到了未来可能如何影响所有人——无论是不是玩家。

Wired: How did you start with computers?  
《连线》：你最初是怎么接触电脑的？

Will Wright: I was involved in building models, which evolved into robots. I got my first computer when I was 20 and taught myself programming to connect to my robots. That's what sucked me into writing software.  
威尔·莱特：我最初是在做模型，后来慢慢发展成了机器人。我二十岁时买了第一台电脑，然后自学编程，让电脑能和我的机器人连接起来。那件事把我吸引进了软件开发的世界。

When did you go from playing around to saying, "I'm going to be a game designer"?  
你是什么时候从“随便玩玩”，变成认真地说“我要成为一名游戏设计师”的？

Around 1980, I was living in New York, and there was one store in the city that sold the Apple II. They had a few simple games and I started thinking, "Maybe I should try making a game, because then I can make my computer expenses tax-deductible." Then I bought a Commodore 64 in 1982, and dedicated myself to learning everything about it.  
大约在 1980 年，我住在纽约。那时全城只有一家店卖 Apple II，店里有几款很简单的游戏。我开始想：“也许我应该试着做游戏，这样我的电脑开销就可以拿去抵税了。”后来我在 1982 年买了一台 Commodore 64，并且一头扎进了对它的研究中。

Has there been a common thread that runs through your career?  
你的职业生涯中，有没有一条始终贯穿其中的线索？

It's really been about trying to construct games around the user. How can you give players more creative leverage and let them show that to other people?  
==其实始终都和围绕用户来构建游戏有关。怎样才能让玩家拥有更多创造性的主动权，并让他们把这种创造展示给其他人看？==

Sim City  
《模拟城市》

That's in your first game, 1984's Raid on Bungeling Bay, but it's almost invisible. It's very action-oriented; you're a helicopter fighting this military-industrial complex. You bomb ships, tanks, planes and factories.  
这一点在你的第一款游戏——1984 年的《Bungeling Bay 突袭》中已经出现了，只是几乎不显眼。那是一款很强调动作性的游戏：你驾驶一架直升机，对抗一个军工复合体。你会轰炸舰船、坦克、飞机和工厂。

How did Bungeling Bay lead to SimCity?  
《Bungeling Bay 突袭》是怎样发展成《模拟城市》的？

I wanted Bungeling Bay to have a world large enough to get lost in -- I was having more fun building worlds than blowing them up.  
我想让《Bungeling Bay 突袭》拥有一个足够庞大的世界，让人可以迷失其中。后来我发现，比起摧毁世界，我从建造世界中得到的乐趣更多。

The game became a blockbuster and passionate fans shared designs online.  
这款游戏后来成为畅销作品，热情的玩家在网上分享他们的设计。

I spent a lot of time studying the community around Quake; I was amazed at the time people were pouring into making their own custom levels. So with The Sims, we wanted to make it possible to modify everything.  
我花了很多时间研究《雷神之锤》周围形成的玩家社区。我很惊讶，人们竟然愿意投入那么多时间制作自己的自定义关卡。所以在做《模拟人生》时，我们希望让一切都可以被修改。

Players could use it as a storytelling platform. At first, the stories were superhero, fantasy stuff. But as the demographics widened, we started getting stories with a message.  
玩家可以把它当作一个讲故事的平台。最开始，大家讲的大多是超级英雄、奇幻之类的故事。但随着玩家群体变得更广泛，我们开始看到一些带有明确表达和主题的故事。

And a lot of the fans were non-traditional gamers, not 18- to 25-year-old males. SimCity had far more female players than most games, by which I mean 20 percent, instead of five percent. But The Sims was predominantly female -- 55 or 60 percent. For a lot of them, it was the only game they played. Plus every few months, we'd come out with an expansion pack for about $30 (£20).  
而且许多玩家并不是传统意义上的游戏玩家，不是十八到二十五岁的男性。《模拟城市》的女性玩家比例远高于大多数游戏——大约是 20%，而不是常见的 5%。但《模拟人生》的玩家则以女性为主，大约占 55% 到 60%。对许多人来说，这甚至是她们唯一会玩的游戏。并且每隔几个月，我们都会推出一个售价约 30 美元、20 英镑左右的资料片。

So they were paying $10 a month for a game that was continually being expanded.  
也就是说，她们相当于每个月花 10 美元，持续为一款不断扩展的游戏付费。

That sounds like the subscription structure of later titles such as World of Warcraft -- which brings to mind 2002's Sims Online.  
这听起来很像后来《魔兽世界》等作品的订阅模式——这也让人想到你们在 2002 年推出的《模拟人生 Online》。

The Sims Online was taking The Sims into a giant, shared world. It was kind of Darwinian: who can build the coolest place that the others want to hang out in?  
《模拟人生 Online》是把《模拟人生》带进一个巨大的共享世界。它有点达尔文主义式的意味：谁能建出最酷的地方，让其他人都想来这里待着？

It lasted only a year, but it foreshadowed things like Second Life, social games such as Farmville -- social networking in general. I wanted the social structures to be as emergent as possible. Players organised into guilds. A group formed a vigilante mob, which became the Sims Online Mafia. There was a godfather, and it turned out to be the woman who managed the housekeeping staff at the Bellagio in Las Vegas. She obviously had organisational skills -- maybe even some familiarity with Mob tactics, I don't know.  
它只维持了一年，但它预示了后来像《第二人生》、像《开心农场》这样的社交游戏，也预示了更广泛意义上的社交网络。我希望其中的社会结构尽可能自然地涌现出来。玩家会自行组织成公会。有一群人组成了一个私刑式团体，后来变成了“模拟人生 Online 黑手党”。里面还有一位“教父”，而那个人后来被发现，是拉斯维加斯贝拉吉奥酒店家政人员的管理者。她显然很擅长组织——至于是否也熟悉黑帮的做事方式，我就不知道了。

Next up was your game Spore, which got a lot of attention for its ambition.  
之后是《孢子》，这款游戏因其宏大的野心而受到了大量关注。

Your creatures start microscopic. They become multicellular. The game moves on to the land, and they become more intelligent. They start having tribal structures, then building cities. They develop spaceflight, and eventually they're discovering other races, terraforming new planets.  
你的生物从微观状态开始。它们变成多细胞生命，接着走上陆地，变得更加聪明。它们开始形成部落结构，之后建造城市，发展太空飞行技术，最终发现其他种族，并对新的星球进行地球化改造。

Now you've focused on Stupid Fun Club.  
现在你的重心已经放在 Stupid Fun Club 上了。

It was originally a playground where we'd build funny, cool things. After I finished Spore, I decided to come to SFC full-time, at which point EA invested in it and it became more like a real company.  
最初，它只是一个让我们做些有趣、酷炫东西的游乐场。完成《孢子》后，我决定全职加入 SFC。后来 EA 对它进行了投资，它也逐渐变得更像一家真正的公司。

What's the mission?  
它的使命是什么？

Taking gaming sensibilities into new areas. Games have been at the front of crowd-sourcing and user-generated content. How do we make TV or whatever more interactive, so that the audience feels ownership?  
把游戏的思维方式带到新的领域中去。游戏一直站在众包和用户生成内容的前沿。我们怎样才能让电视或其他媒介变得更加互动，让观众也感觉到自己拥有其中的一部分？

What will happen with games?  
游戏未来会变成什么样？

I don't think anybody can look two years out and have a clue what the industry is going to look like, which is exciting.  
我不觉得有人能够看清两年之后这个行业会变成什么样，而这正是令人兴奋的地方。

The social-gaming market is attracting the untapped group that The Sims found. That really broadens the types of games that we can create.  
社交游戏市场正在吸引《模拟人生》当年发现的那批尚未被开发的玩家群体。这真正拓宽了我们能够创造的游戏类型。

Now, a game launches and people like one feature, so the developers tear the game up and build everything around that feature. It used to be that companies would commit years and millions of dollars to a game, then roll the dice and release it.  
现在，一款游戏上线后，如果人们喜欢其中某个功能，开发者就会把游戏拆开，围绕那个功能重新构建一切。过去，公司会花几年时间、投入数百万美元做一款游戏，然后像掷骰子一样把它发布出去，看看结果如何。

Now it's: "Let's do it slowly based on audience reaction." We can get feedback within hours and change the game while they're playing. We can begin customising games for individuals.  
而现在则是：“让我们根据玩家反应，慢慢来做。”我们可以在几个小时内得到反馈，并在他们游玩的过程中改变游戏。我们开始能够为不同的人定制游戏。

What would a game like that look like?  
那样的游戏会是什么样子？

Imagine Steven Spielberg talking to you, observing what you watched, and then going off to make a movie just for you. It's a little scary: we're talking about something potentially more addictive than any drug.  
想象一下，史蒂文·斯皮尔伯格和你交谈，观察你看了什么，然后转身去为你一个人拍一部电影。这有一点可怕：我们讨论的东西，可能比任何药物都更容易让人上瘾。

Does this relate to games that Stupid Fun Club is developing now?  
这和 Stupid Fun Club 现在正在开发的游戏有关吗？

I can't talk much about them, but one is about how to turn your life into a game. Your real life, not a virtual fantasy world. The other one is about taking all your digital content and making it feel tangible and playful, a window into the cloud of your stuff.  
我不能多谈，但其中一个项目和怎样把你的生活变成一场游戏有关。是真实生活，不是虚拟的幻想世界。另一个则是把你所有的数字内容变得更具实体感、更有玩耍的感觉，让它成为一扇通往你个人云端内容的窗口。

You think data visualisation is going to play a bigger role in life?  
你认为数据可视化会在生活中扮演更重要的角色吗？

It's a literacy that people will start developing, now we're able to capture so much personal information.  
这会成为一种人们逐渐具备的素养。现在我们已经能够记录如此大量的个人信息。

You're going to have an interest in interpreting that. It's about people finding ways to get insight into themselves.  
人们会开始有兴趣理解这些信息。关键在于，人们会借此找到了解自己、看见自己的方式。

The Sims  
《模拟人生》

Both of these concepts seem to be about interactivity becoming more and more ingrained in what we do.  
这两个概念似乎都和互动性越来越深地融入我们的生活有关。

Storytelling is around you all the time -- games are evolving to have the same pervasiveness in our lives.  
故事无时无刻不在我们身边——游戏也正在演变，逐渐拥有同样深入日常生活的普遍性。
